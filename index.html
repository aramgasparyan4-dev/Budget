Real Estate Capital

A property game where the economy is real: unequal starts, one open market, taxes, mortgages, global events, and the occasional trip to the emergency room.

Players

How it works

  • Unequal starts. Each player is dealt a background: a trust-fund heir with inherited towers and estate tax, a nurse with one kid, a graduate with student debt. You play the hand you are dealt.
  • One market. Every property has a live value driven by the economy, its district, and its condition. Buy unowned buildings when you land on them (or off-market at a 5% premium), list your own for sale, or make offers on anyone's.
  • Months, not laps. One round of turns is one month. Salary, rent, mortgage payments, maintenance, property tax, and premiums settle every month. Income tax is due every December.
  • Landing fees. Land on someone's property and you pay a month of rent. Own a whole district and rents jump 25%.
  • Manage or decay. Buildings wear out. Repair them, renovate them, insure them, set rents low or high. Neglect costs occupancy, value, and eventually fines.
  • Global events. Recessions, rate hikes, pandemics, hurricanes, rent control, tech booms. Read the news.
  • Health. Age and stress wear you down. Medical emergencies cost tens of thousands without insurance and can put you out of action for months.
  • Debt. Short on cash? Your credit line kicks in at a brutal rate. Run out of credit and your assets get seized. Highest net worth when time runs out wins.
